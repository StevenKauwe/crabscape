## 1. The rule that builds the list: no verb, no skill

A skill exists **only where a canon verb already exists** to grow in
(`vision` "skills grow by use"; C3). Skills are the *long-run measure* of
repeated verb use — never a parallel system with its own verbs. Consequence:
anything not on the skill list below is not "missing a skill"; it is a verb
that isn't canon yet.

## 3. XP and the two books (axis 2 — position taken; hit it)

**Position, named per #3's standard: option (a) — skills bank by doing.**
XP accrues in the water, continuously, tick at the moment (C12's own
wording: skills grow by use); death costs only the dive-book. No XP loss, no
XP-at-bank, no hedge.

- **Levels *land* at the shell** — the threshold crosses mid-dive, but the
  level-up beat plays at home, during the mundane verb (C11: bank, then
  cook/repair — the new level is on the readout when you next check). This
  marries no-loss with legibility (C6): the level lands where levels are
  *pointable*.
  Disambiguated (FS-1): **credit is instant at the moment of use; ceremony
  is deferred to the shell; death forfeits nothing but the delay.** The
  underwater HUD never shows XP or level numbers — in the water, progress
  is body-pointable (the +8% is in your legs); the number exists only at
  home (architecture F1).
- **Where death sting scales for the skilled player** (the obligation #3
  attached to option (a)): **cargo-depth scaling.** Climbing/stroking/
  sensing levels make deeper comfortable dives routine; deeper dives mean
  richer carries; richer carries mean bigger moults to lose (Cmd 10, Cmd
  12). The skilled player risks more per dive because their competence
  buys access to heavier cargo — not because their past is held hostage.
  Knowledge remains the only currency death pays (synthesis/01 §4), by
  design.
- **Practice vs milestone (adopting #6's S-1, with one correction):** in-dive
  fluency is *performance, not currency* — the reserve, the rhythm window,
  the winded state are the verb's own costs and reset each dive by design;
  there is no dive-book XP to lose, so C2 is never mixed because nothing
  needs converting at the bank. The only persistent skill state is
  home-book, and it banks live. (Correction to S-1's phrasing: practice is
  not "dive-book" — it is not a book at all; books hold value, and
  in-dive fluency is valueless the moment it ends. Calling it dive-book
  would re-import XP-loss through the back door.)
- **The three-jurisdiction currency** (mirroring #6's v1.4 pointer, composed
  from #3's requirement): *keys claim-then-bank / fluency banks-by-doing /
  perception banks-by-doing (C7's own progression kind — not a verb grant);
  gates check the perceiving or the doing, never the claiming.* Sensing
  unlocks therefore cannot be smuggled through claim-then-bank: the
  perceiving is its own progression kind.
- **Why not XP-loss-on-death** (the counter argued at its strongest): the
  loss that matters already has perfect teeth — cargo stays in the moult
  (Cmd 10, Cmd 12), and knowledge is what death pays (synthesis/01 §4).
  Adding XP-loss strikes the same moment twice, and the second strike buys
  no decision (C1): re-earning spent XP is repetition without choice — the
  literal definition of a chore. It also teaches risk-aversion in a game
  whose pitch is dive-forward. *Strongest counter*, stated fairly: Valheim
  taxes skill XP on death and it reads as brutal-but-fair there — but
  Valheim's death has no rescue-dive hook; ours does. The moult *is* our
  death tax, and it's retrievable. **Resolved: XP banks live. No XP loss.
  The sea keeps cargo, not competence.** [Owner-facing note, not a hedge:
  the hardcore option is explicitly later-mode (`vision`); if it ever
  wants a Valheim tax, that is a toggle on top of this ruling, not this
  ruling.] [GAP-flag for owner: axis-2 ruling is draft-form pending your
  veto in review.]

### SYS-F1 resolution — two currencies, two laws (accepted; the MAJOR was real)

The collision was inside my own §2/§4 all along: **new verbs are
claim-then-bank (gates, C3); fluency banks-by-doing (feel, never gates).**
The egg and the climb-length were never the same currency. Eggs (and every
verb-granting found-thing) are *keys* — keys are cargo-class, they ride the
back, and they obey the survival law: claim in place, convert only at home
(Cmd 12). Fluency numbers (climb +8%, sensing discrimination, cooking
quality) are *competence* — weak currency per C3, they bank by doing,
survive death, and may never gate. "The sea keeps cargo, not competence"
keeps its punchline *because* competence was never the thing in the belly.
Both rules now hold in the same twenty minutes without contradiction:
eat an egg + die = the stroke stays with the sea (Cmd 12); climb + die = the
strength in your legs is yours (bank-live).

**Softness worry (SYS-F2), answered twice:** meta kept light keeps runs
honest — that is the Dead Cells warning the bible already carries (bible
Q2), and the post-mastery dive contract (synthesis/01 §2.4) is the veteran's
self-set difficulty valve, so a lossless progression never means a frictionless
endgame.

## 4. Curves are feel, gates are verbs (axis 3 — the C3 tension resolved)

Core's hint, made law: **numeric curves tune feel; gates open to verbs and
perception, never to raw numbers.**

- `+8%/level` Climbing survives *as feel*: the number exists so mastery is
  visible in the body (C6 — pointable: name what got easier after a level;
  if nothing, the level is a chore-number → cut or bundle [C1]).
- **No gate in the game may check "Climbing ≥ 7."** **The unified gate law**
  (#2 SK-A + lens L-2, merged): a gate is legal only if it is
  (a) **VERB-class** — you own the verb (scissor claws cut the net);
  (b) **PERCEPTION-class** — it changes what you can notice (C7; the alcove
  pattern); or
  (c) **DA-class** — which requires BOTH named real chemistry
  (`ocean-shaped` TMAO) AND the lethality test (failing the gate kills).
  **Corollary (SK-A): route feasibility fails (a) and (c) simultaneously —
  geometry may never gate on curves.** A wall sized to a curve threshold is
  a verb-check you don't own wearing a number; curves modulate comfort and
  time, never feasibility.
- **The one sanctioned capacity gate:** depth bands. `world` is explicit
  that pressure/cold/oxygen are real limits met by "preparing and adapting,
  not only by levelling" — so Deep-adaptation may gate *survivable depth*,
  because the bible itself names the limit. Trained entirely by verbs
  (forage→cook→eat). This edge exists **only** because `ocean-shaped`
  grounds it (TMAO, body chemistry); no other number-gate gets this
  courtesy. **Anti-crack wall (SYS, adopted): no second capacity gate
  without named real chemistry — a feeling is not a chemistry.** Any future
  capacity gate must cite a real mechanism in `ocean-shaped`
  (`~/Work/crabscape/docs/design/ocean-shaped.md`) or `universe.md`
  (`~/Work/crabscape-bestiary/docs/design/universe.md` — full path, per the
  auditor's F-3 lesson) — or it does not exist.

**Two more walls (adopted — architecture F2, lens L-2):**
- **Graded vs binary:** curves SCALE a state's risk margin; they never FLIP
  pass/fail. A climbing level widens what you can recover from in a surge —
  it does not make a lethal state survivable as a gate. Any number that
  flips pass/fail is a gate and falls back under this section's law.
- **Lethality is the capacity-gate test:** Deep-adaptation is DA-class
  because failing pressure/cold *kills*; light merely stops you (you turn
  back alive), so light-radius gates are Sensing-class (C7) — the carve-out
  cannot generalize to anything that doesn't kill.

**Proposed Cmd 13:** *Curves are feel; gates are verbs or perception. Curves
scale risk margins and never flip pass/fail; a capacity gate exists only
where failing it is lethal and the chemistry is named. If a level can't be
pointed at, cut it.* (C3, C6, C7; graded-vs-binary and lethality clauses
from architecture F2 and lens L-2.)

## 5. The web keeps old water load-bearing (axes 4+5)

Mechanisms, each grounded — **the Valheim law (new zones never retire old
ones) is enforced by all four at once:**

1. **Every zone best-trains some skill.** Sunlit kelp = the climbing gym;
   twilight currents = stroke-rhythm school; midnight dark = sensing
   pressure; the trench = deep-adaptation table. Training grounds are
   distributed, not stacked [`world` zone table].
2. **Endgame sinks consume low-zone resources** [`world` "things get used
   up"]: high-tier crafts and rations need shallow bulk (kelp as stew base,
   beach glass as culture ware). Veterans return to zone 1 as *suppliers*,
   not tourists. [GAP-6: exact sink recipes need the economy pass — owner.]
   GAP-6 spec constraints (architecture F5, FS-5): sink demand must
   **compete for the single carry slot** — a sink run is a loadout decision
   (what to carry home), never a checklist quota ("bring 20 kelp" is Skyrim
   potions with extra steps); and every sink declares its **visit pattern**
   (one-pass vs recurring, recurrence justified by a rotation layer) — the
   treadmill test, institutionalized now rather than retrofitted later.
3. **Re-entry secrets (Hollow Knight grammar):** every later verb/perception
   grant re-keys at least one lock in every older zone — the alcove pattern
   (`world`) generalized: new light, old rock. [GAP-flag: density formula
   owner-set; draft suggests ≥1 per zone per major verb grant.]
4. **Route-table synergy:** skills change which passage states you can
   *exploit* (synthesis/01 §2.4: 2–3 states per named passage) — a sensing
   level reveals a state earlier; a climbing level widens the recovery
   margin in a surge state (risk margin scales; pass/fail never flips — §4).
   Fixed geometry (C5) + rotating states (Cmd 4) + growing verbs
   = the same dozen passages play differently for months.

**The revisit test (campaign go-criteria, same style as §6 of the loop
proposal):** *for each new zone shipped, name reasons a veteran returns to
every older zone — a sink, a secret, a training ground. If any old zone has
none, the new zone is a leaver; fix it before ship.* **Runtime gauge (SYS-F6,
adopted):** the test is design-time; C12 is verified at runtime by the
**old-zone visit rate** in telemetry (C9) — if visit rate decays to zero for
any zone in steady-state play, the web has broken and the next patch owns
the fix. (C12.)
Reasons are classified **durative vs one-shot** (architecture F4): sinks are
durative (recurring demand keeps a zone load-bearing); secrets are one-shot
(bonuses). When the test is scored, durative reasons weigh more.

**Proposed Cmd 14 (SYS-F7 standard applied — ≥1, not 3-each):** *The web
keeps old water load-bearing: every new zone ships with at least one new
reason — sink, secret, or training ground — for every older zone.* (C12,
C5.) The three categories are a menu, not a quota; the binding standard is
the revisit test plus the visit-rate gauge.

**Co-op note (SYS-F5):** skills are per-player — muscle and knowledge are
mortal books; sinks and buildings are shared (precedent: synthesis/01 §4).

## 8. BIOMASS — one currency, three source profiles (owner concept; Cmd 15/16)

**Biomass is one stackable currency with three source profiles:**

| Profile | Verb | Risk | Attention | Rate | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HUNT** | kill (claw styles) | front-loaded — the fight IS the cost | full | fastest per session | [`how-it-plays` combat; **GAP-1**] |
| **SCAVENGE** | claim carrion | deferred — the plume summons the web (C12/Cmd-8 class risk: the carcass is a beacon) | *reallocated*, not idle: you park near a kill and watch the water | fast per unit, patchy supply | [talks/ carrion convergence; `world`] |
| **FARM** | tend/harvest the kelp garden (home) | none — risk-zero, home-adjacent | low: plant, leave, return | deliberately **slowest** per session | [`vision` cozy pillar; `world` kelp] |

These are **playstyle identities, not difficulty tiers** (#1 framing, adopted):
hunter, scavenger, farmer — same currency, three risk/attention contracts,
all valid paths to the same molt. **Presentation law (B1/B5, adopted):** the
profiles are design taxonomy — never labels, badges, or titles in-game (a
'Hunter' title IS a tier; the shell expresses identity, the UI never names
it), and **rates are never surfaced comparatively** — any yield-comparison
UI collapses the identities into an efficiency table this axis exists to
prevent.

**The rations overlap, resolved:** biomass has two *forms* of the same unit.
**Raw** biomass banks at home = **molt fuel** (the body's cost, Cmd 16).
**Rations** = prepared biomass carried as **dive fuel** (eaten under
pressure, spent — never molt-fuel). One currency, two spend decisions, and
the home-tier decision the owner named: *of my bank, how much becomes
rations for the next dive, and how much stays raw for the molt?* Cooking's
loop role is now structural: a ration is **cargo-dense** — more fuel per
back-slot than raw — so the cooker trades home time for dive endurance
(C1: every conversion is a choice). **ML-3 RULED by the owner (supersedes
the v1 inert ruling): raw biomass is edible anytime, anywhere — no
artificial penalty.** The trade is structural, not penal: raw = savings
spent on the present (inefficient); rations = efficient dive fuel, and
**Cooking improves the conversion** — the skill's payoff is now exactly
"more dive-endurance per back-slot." Eating raw mid-dive is the emergency
valve and the impatient choice; cooking is the optimizer's ritual. Both
legal; only one is *smart*. **Presentation (architecture R1, adopted):**
raw-eating underwater is a diegetic chomp — felt, slow, never a menu
action; the felt slowness is what keeps the cook's door smart rather than
merely efficient.

**The four-state biomass map (T-3, adopted — without it the ledger leaks):**

## 10. MOLT GATING — cadence, ceremony, and where the vulnerability lives

- **Cadence is the pacing curve:** molts are frequent and cheap early
  (tiny crab, small meals), rare and expensive late (big milestones, big
  biomass) — diminishing returns as *body cost*, not a stat curve (C3:
  feats still grant verbs/perception only; size is presentation and body
  cost, never a gate number). [knowledge: real decapod molt increments
  shrink with size]
- **The prepaid molt (owner ruling — premolt anorexia, documented in the
  crab-ocean world bible):** you can eat anytime — *until you commit.*
  Committing to a molt locks eating: the molt is prepaid, and the body's
  reserve must be bought before the lock (the documented pre-molt feeding
  surge is the mechanism). Post-hardening, the first meal is the C11
  resumption beat — structurally guaranteed, not hoped for. Commitment is
  a real decision twice over: when to stop eating and start the clock, and
  whether the reserve covers anorexia + ecdysis + hardening.
  **The commit must be unmistakable and diegetic (architecture R3,
  adopted):** the prepaid contract is stated at the beat — the crab stops
  eating and the player knows why; no UI path commits by misclick.
  Lockout cruelty is designed, never accidental.
- **The chase and the commitment are the two turn-around-class decisions**
  (#4 axis, retained and extended): the threshold is a stated goal — X
  biomass — a dive with a known target and a known reason to push or turn.
  The commitment adds the second clock: push one encounter deeper, or
  convert now — and *commit now, because the eating window is closing.*
- **The three-tier molt ladder (owner ruling — supersedes 'home only';
  vulnerability is OPTED INTO, risk bought with reach, C1-clean).
