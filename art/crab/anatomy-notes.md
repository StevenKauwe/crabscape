# Player crab: anatomy checklist

Read this before you draw or animate the crab. It lists what the drawing has to get right. Each fact names the source
it came from, and every source here was opened during research on 2026-10-15. Where a fact couldn't be checked in a
source, it says so.

## Checklist

### Body plan
- [x] **Ten legs.** A decapod has 5 pairs of pereiopods, "the ten legs from which decapods take their name" [WP-PER].
  In true crabs, "the first of which is chelate (ending in pincers) and nearly always much stronger than the other
  legs" [FAO-TC]. That gives **1 pair of claws (chelipeds) and 4 pairs of walking legs**. FAO's dorsal plate numbers
  the walking legs 1 to 4 [FAO-TC].
  **Count the legs in every view: 2 claws + 8 legs.** In a view where some legs are hidden, the note says which.
- [x] **Where the legs attach.** The coxa, basis and ischium are "small segments which attach the limb to the body of
  the crab" [FC-LEG]. All the legs come out from under the edge of the carapace, along the sides of the underside
  (the FAO dorsal plate shows them emerging beneath the margin) [FAO-TC].
- [x] **Leg segments,** from the body outward: coxa, basis, ischium, merus, carpus, propodus, dactylus [WP-LEG]. In many
  decapods some of these fuse; the FAO plate labels a "fused basis-ischium" on the cheliped [FAO-TC]. The **merus is
  the largest segment** [FC-LEG].
- [x] **Joints are one-plane hinges.** Each segment "articulates with the next segment in a hinge joint and may only bend
  in one plane" [WP-LEG]. Crab leg joints "are simple hinges, like our knees, and they only bend sideways" [SF].
  The drawing has to follow this: a walking leg bends in the vertical plane that runs crosswise to the body. The
  merus rises outward to a high "knee" (the merus–carpus joint), then the carpus, propodus and dactyl come down to a
  pointed dactyl tip. **No leg ever bends forward or back at the knee in the front view.**
- [x] **Sideways walking.** The wide, flat body "restricts the range of motion at the 'shoulder' joint", so crabs
  "move much faster by flexing the second joint of each leg" sideways [SF].
- [~] **Walking gait, partly verified.** Barnes (1975), "Leg co-ordination during walking in the crab, *Uca pugnax*",
  *J. Comp. Physiol.* 96:237–256. I opened only its bibliographic entry (fiddlercrab.info/references/Barnes1975.1.html).
  The Springer abstract was behind a login, and its summary reached me through a search snippet, not a page I read:
  the average gait is "close to an alternating tetrapod rhythm", the stepping order differs between the leading and
  trailing sides, and the claw is held off the ground.
  **How we use it:** two sets of four legs alternate, taking diagonals across the left and right sides. Set A is
  left 1 and 3 with right 2 and 4; set B is right 1 and 3 with left 2 and 4. The legs on the leading side reach out
  and pull, the trailing side pushes, and the claws never touch the ground.

### Claws (chelipeds)
- [x] **Fixed finger and movable finger.** The claw "is formed by the articulation of the dactylus against an outgrowth
  of the propodus" [WP-LEG]. The segment before the movable finger "houses the muscles" and carries the fixed finger
  [WP-CHE]. "The moveable fingers of a claw are known as dactyli" [WP-DEC]. FAO labels the parts hand/palm, dactylus,
  carpus with an inner carpal spine, and merus [FAO-TC].
  **How we draw it:** the palm and the fixed finger are one rigid part (`propodus`), and the dactyl is the only finger
  that moves. Crab claws close dactyl down onto the fixed finger, so the fixed finger is the lower one.
- [x] **Unequal claws (heterochely) are real.** Many crabs have a heavy crusher claw and a slimmer cutter claw. The green
  crab has both: "The mechanical advantages of C. maenas crusher and cutter claws are 0.37 and 0.30" [YDF]. In the
  stone crab the "pincers [are] distinctly unequal… fingers meeting only at tips, more gaping in larger hand" [FAO-TC].
  - *Not verified:* a search snippet said the crusher is usually on the right. I could not open that paper
    (JEB 218:3658, HTTP 403), so the claim stays unverified. Putting our crusher on the crab's right is a design
    choice.

### Head end
- [x] **Eyes on stalks, in sockets (orbits) in the front edge.** FAO labels the eyestalk, cornea and orbit [FAO-TC].
  One crab's long eyestalks "may be folded back into an indentation in the shell" [CALTECH]. Glossaries describe a
  "groove close to and parallel with margin of orbit" [NAT-JP]. How far the eyes fold away differs by family, and the
  FAO keys separate families partly by orbit shape [FAO-TC].
  The *Carcinus* eye-withdrawal reflex study (J. Exp. Biol. 46:475) came back HTTP 403, so I couldn't read it.
  Folding the eyes is still safe to show, because [CALTECH] supports it.
- [x] **Two pairs of antennae between the eyes.** FAO's ventral plate shows the **antennules** (small, folding into the
  antennular fossae) and the **antennae** (with a flagellum) between and beside the orbits [FAO-TC]. [WP-DEC] lists
  antennules and antennae as the first two head appendages.
- [x] **Mouthparts.** The third maxillipeds are broadened and flattened "to form a more or less complete operculum over
  the other mouth parts" (NHM glossary, via its definitions page [NHM]; the operculum wording reached me through a
  search snippet). FAO's frontal plate labels the "3rd maxilliped" over the "buccal cavern" [FAO-TC]. For ghost crabs,
  FAO says the "third maxillipeds usually completely cover[] mouth cavity" [FAO-TC].
  **How we draw it:** two squarish plates meet in the middle like double doors, low on the front face below the eyes.

### Body
- [x] **The abdomen is small and folded under.** True crabs have "a much reduced, straight, and symmetrical abdomen which
  is closely bent under the cephalothorax; this abdomen is never used for swimming" [FAO-TC]. In the blue crab, males
  have a "T-shaped apron" and mature females a "bell-shaped" one [CQ], [WP-CS]. **No tail is visible from above.**
- [x] **The carapace is broad and toothed.** Portunids have a "carapace broad and flat, with 5 to 9 teeth on
  anterolateral margin; well-developed lateral spine at greatest width" [FAO-TC]. The blue crab has 4 frontal teeth
  between the eyes [WP-CS].
- [x] **Gills sit under the carapace.** The carapace "protects the internal organs and gills", and every appendage from
  the second maxilla to the fifth pereiopod carries a gill [WP-DEC]. [CQ] calls the gills "dead man's fingers".
  **Crabs breathe water, so the crab gets no air tank and no goggles.**

### Swimming legs (Portunidae)
- [x] Portunids are "characterised by the flattening of the fifth pair of legs into broad paddles" [WP-POR], with the
  "last pair of legs usually distinctly adapted for swimming, flattened and paddle-shaped" [FAO-TC]. The FAO key
  separates *Carcinus* ("distal articles of fifth legs not paddle-like") from the paddle-legged genera [FAO-TC].
  **Recommendation: YES.** It is real biology and it gives the game its jump, double jump and swim (confirmed by the
  owner on 2026-10-15). The paddles are the last two segments (propodus and dactyl) flattened into an oval blade.
  - *Not verified:* that blue crabs carry their paddles raised over the back while they walk. I remember seeing
    this, but no source I opened says so. In the drawing the paddles sit just above the rear corners of the
    carapace. A biologist should check this.

### Moulting, lost limbs and display
- [x] **A fresh moult is soft.** "A crab immediately after shedding its old shell; its new shell is soft and pliable"
  [CQ]. It reaches paper shell at 9–12 h and buckram (semi-hard) at 12–24 h [CQ]. Blue crabs moult about 25 times in a
  lifetime [WP-CS].
  - *Not verified:* that the new shell looks **paler**. It is widely reported, but I didn't find it in any source I
    opened. Here it is an artistic reading, used because it reads well.
- [x] **Lost limbs regrow over moults, and the new one is different at first.** In fiddler crabs, regenerated claws
  "were more slender, having smaller dimensions of the manus relative to claw length", and the first moult after the
  loss accounted for most of the change [KM]. A bluecrab.info page on autotomy (HTTP 403) and a search snippet say
  the new limb starts as a small "limb bud" and needs 1–3 moults to reach full size. That part is not verified from a
  page I read.
  The drawing shows a small, slender claw in place of the crusher. One step is shown, and the stages after it are not
  drawn.
- [~] **Threat display: claws raised and spread wide.** It is widely described. The study I found (Royal Holloway
  repository; it failed the TLS check) said in a snippet that "chelipeds are extended and raised, and strikes come
  only from that position". I could not open it, so this counts as partly verified.

### Light and colour
- [x] **Red goes black at depth.** "Red light is quickly filtered from water as depth increases… At 100 meters, red light
  does not penetrate… the fish appears blackish because there is no red light to reflect" [NOAA].
- [x] **Lantern bacteria.** Most luminous bacteria glow blue-green. *Vibrio fischeri* strain Y-1 "emits yellow light
  rather than the blue-green emission typical of other luminous bacteria". Its yellow fluorescent protein adds a peak
  near 534 nm to the luciferase's 484 nm [DAUB]. The light needs oxygen, and the bacteria only glow once there are
  enough of them (quorum sensing) [WP-AF].
  **Design use:** the crab farms a Y-1-like **yellow strain** in its jar, so the lantern glows warm and that is real
  biology. Wild bioluminescence elsewhere stays cyan or violet, which fits the colour key. A dim lantern can mean the
  jar needs fresh oxygenated water, which is a possible game mechanic.

### Side-scroller reasoning (checked)
- A crab walks sideways [SF], and the side-on game camera looks across the direction of travel. So a crab walking left
  or right across the screen **shows the camera its front: eyes, mouthparts and both claws.** The main in-game view
  is therefore the **front view**, and the whole sheet is built around it.
  Turning to walk the other way needs no mirrored "back" sprite, because crabs walk equally well to either side. The
  crusher stays on the crab's right, which is screen left in every frame. That keeps the asymmetry honest, and the
  game never needs a flipped sprite.

### Found objects (grounded, though not checked in sources this session)
- A bottle-cap helmet held on by hooked setae, as decorator crabs (Majoidea) do with debris. A sea-glass shield
  carried in the cutter claw, the way boxer crabs (*Lybia*) hold anemones. Both rest on well-known behaviour, but I
  didn't open a source for either this session. Check them before writing lore.
